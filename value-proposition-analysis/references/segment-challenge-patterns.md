# Segment challenge patterns

A starter lookup of common market segments and the pain points that show up
most often in each one. Use this only after you know the target segment.
Match loosely on the segment name the user gives you (for example
"small business" or "solo founder" both map to SMB / solopreneur).

This is a starter set, not a full taxonomy. When the user names a segment
that isn't here, don't guess. Ask them directly what that segment's biggest
challenges are, then use their answer instead of this table.

## SMB / solopreneur

- Limited time, one person often covers several jobs at once.
- Tight budget, every tool has to earn its cost fast.
- No dedicated IT or ops staff to set things up or fix them.
- Needs to see value quickly, a long ramp-up kills adoption.
- Manual, repetitive work eats hours that should go to the business itself.

## Mid-market

- Growing past the point where spreadsheets and manual process hold up.
- Multiple teams need the same data and keep it in sync themselves.
- Budget exists, but has to be justified with a clear return.
- Some in-house technical staff, but not enough to build everything custom.
- Choosing between several vendors that each solve part of the problem.

## Enterprise

- Security, compliance, and procurement review before anything gets bought.
- Integration with existing systems is often the actual blocker, not price.
- Multiple stakeholders and departments need to sign off.
- Rollout has to work at scale, across many teams or locations at once.
- Existing tools and legacy systems are hard to move away from.

## Fintech

- Regulatory and compliance requirements shape almost every decision.
- Data accuracy and auditability matter as much as speed.
- Security review is heavier than in most other verticals.
- Existing systems are often older and harder to integrate with.
- Trust with end customers depends on getting money-related details right.

## Healthcare

- Patient data privacy and compliance (for example HIPAA in the US) gate
  what a tool is even allowed to touch.
- Staff are often stretched thin, so a tool has to save real time to matter.
- Systems of record are frequently older and slow to change.
- Approval often needs sign-off from both clinical and administrative sides.
- Errors carry a higher cost than in most other verticals, so trust in
  accuracy is a bigger factor in the buying decision.
